# Jobs, schedules, and workflow execution

## Scope and data model

Jobs and schedules MUST have stable definitions, canonical execution scope, bounded retry, and versioned payload/progress where durable. Claims MUST carry fencing identity; a stale or expired owner MUST NOT update progress, complete, or reschedule work.

Job identity/fingerprint binds definition, version, scoped payload, and idempotency namespace where applicable. Durable status transitions are explicit (pending/claimable, running, retryable, succeeded, terminal failure/cancelled as supported). Progress is monotonic according to the job contract and written only by current claim.

The job fingerprint (version `5`) also binds the per-job attempt budget: an omitted `maxAttempts` (the job uses its definition's budget, persisted as the definition sentinel) is encoded distinctly from every explicit number, so an idempotent re-enqueue replays only a job with the same budget semantics. Stored version-4 and version-3 fingerprints, which treated an omitted budget as 3, are still recognised (compatibility-only) and match either form.

Job store claim and transition semantics (verified against the real PostgreSQL, MySQL and D1 stores by the job-store differential): every terminal status (`succeeded`, `failed`, `dead_letter`, `cancelled`) is sticky, so `cancel` of a terminal job is rejected with `INVALID_STATE` and leaves the job unchanged. `claimDue` with a limit that is not a positive safe integer claims nothing (it never means "unbounded"). The jobs of one partition key are serialized per scope and tenant: a live running job blocks only the jobs of its own scope and tenant, a batch offers at most the best-ranked claimable job of each partition (a busy or backlogged partition MUST NOT occupy the limit and starve jobs behind it), and a running job whose lease lapsed is still reclaimable. A claim stamps `startedAt` with the `now` it was given; due-ness of pending and retrying jobs is judged against that `now`, lease liveness on the database clock. A renewal moves the lease expiry forward only: a shorter extension than the remaining lease leaves the longer lease in place. Failure text that contains U+0000 is recorded (a store whose text type cannot hold it replaces it with U+FFFD), never rejected.

## Dispatch, retry, and scheduling

Dispatch MUST honor cancellation, deadline, retry policy, and idempotency. Schedule lifecycle transitions and next-run calculation MUST be deterministic for the declared time basis. Payload migration MUST be explicit and reject unknown unsupported versions.

Retry policy bounds attempts and delay and MUST NOT exceed enclosing expiry/deadline. Schedules define timezone/time basis, cadence, missed-run behavior, overlap policy, and enabled lifecycle. A schedule claim's previous `lastStatus` of `running` is the store's claim-lease marker (for example left by a crashed worker), not execution state: whether an earlier occurrence is still busy under a `skip` overlap policy is decided only by the prior-job lookup for that schedule (within one claim's `queue_all` backlog, by the current status of the job already stored for the preceding occurrence: queued or running is busy, any terminal status is not). Concurrent dispatchers MUST not both commit ownership for one occurrence. Job, schedule, and workflow lease renewals for one claim MUST be serialized, atomically verify the fencing token, move expiry monotonically forward, and settle before the dispatcher releases its renewal lifecycle. A pending renewal MUST settle before successful job/workflow completion can commit.

Calendar exclusion applies per occurrence. A single-run policy (`fire_now`, `skip`) is skipped when its claimed occurrence is excluded and advances to the first occurrence after now. In a `queue_all` backlog each occurrence is checked on its own: an excluded occurrence is consumed without a job (and without using a remaining occurrence) and the others are enqueued; a backlog that is entirely excluded advances past its last occurrence with status `skipped`. Cron calendar arithmetic MUST treat years 0-99 as those years, not as 1900-1999.

Schedule store claim semantics (verified against the real PostgreSQL, MySQL and D1 stores by the schedule-store differential): a schedule held by a live claim is not claimable and MUST NOT use up another worker's claim `limit` (the candidate scan excludes it, not only the conditional update), claims are returned oldest-due first, and `renewScheduleLease` moves the lease expiry forward only: a renewal with a shorter extension than the remaining lease leaves the longer lease in place. Due-ness, effective window, remaining occurrences and failure backoff are judged against the caller's `now`; lease liveness is judged on the database clock. A schedule advanced with `nextRunAt: null` (no future occurrence) is retired and never claimed again, which requires a nullable `next_run_at` column.

## Errors and conformance

Unknown definitions/payload versions are validation/configuration failures; stale tokens conflict; handler failures follow retry policy; failure text handed to a store (`lastError`, release `reason`) is bounded to 16 KiB UTF-8 on every path (handler, payload decode, scope, retry policy, attempt limit, unknown definition), truncated with a `... [truncated]` marker; store failures preserve claim/fencing; a failure while setting up one claimed job (for example a throwing id generator or clock) is contained to that job and MUST NOT prevent the other jobs claimed in the same batch from running. The job is handed back with `release` (no attempt consumed, retried no sooner than one lease duration later) and reported as `retried`; if the store cannot release it, it is reported as `lease_lost` and its lease lapses. A job with no runnable definition is likewise released; a transient release failure is retried once before the attempt-consuming fallback is used. Hand-back time: the next attempt time of every job handed back without consuming an attempt (a release for a setup failure or an unknown definition, and the `markRetrying` fallback of an unknown-definition deferral) is the dispatcher clock reading taken immediately before that store call is issued, plus exactly one lease duration. It is never carried over from an earlier failed call or clamped against it, so a retried release is stamped from the clock at the retry, however slow the failed call was and whether or not the clock has since moved backwards. Worker and definition configuration (dispatcher limits, lease and timeout durations, job definitions, retry policies, schedule lease and `maxQueueAllOccurrences`) is invalid with `ConfigurationError`, never `ValidationError`, which is reserved for request input. Conformance covers fingerprint stability, claim races, lease expiry/reclaim, stale progress/completion, retry exhaustion, payload migration, schedule boundaries/overlap, cancellation, and process termination/restart.

Traceability: `packages/core/src/execution`; PG/D1/MySQL job and schedule suites; L1/J1 certification.
